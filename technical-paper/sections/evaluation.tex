\section{Completion Outpaces Score Coverage}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Protocol.} Three hosted endpoints process the same balanced
\CorpusTaskCount{}-task suite under one agent revision. Each completed
output must pass \CSim, \Synth, 100\,MHz, U55C capacity, and applicable
\CoSim{} under the Track-A contract~\cite{fpt26tracka}. The models span
three architecture--scale regimes: DeepSeek V4 Pro (1.6\,T MoE, 49\,B
active), Qwen3.5-122B-A10B (122\,B MoE, 10\,B active), and Qwen3.6-27B
(27\,B dense).

\textbf{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:headline-results} summarises the three
campaigns. Qwen3.6-27B achieves \QwenThreeSixSuccessRate{} completion
(\QwenThreeSixSuccessCount{}/\CorpusTaskCount{} tasks,
\QwenThreeSixFailureCount{} failures), including 25/25 in the challenging
structural \CoSim{} repair category. Despite being the smallest model, it
outperforms both larger MoE models on completion and optimization quality.
Qwen3.5-122B-A10B is the most token-efficient
(\QwenThreeFiveTokensM{}M tokens), while DeepSeek V4 Pro consumes
\DeepSeekTokensM{}M tokens (3.1$\times$ more than Qwen3.6) with no
measurable QoR improvement.

Per-category breakdown (Appendix~\ref{app:results}) reveals four patterns.
(1)~Only explicit QoR optimization tasks yield scores above baseline; all
five repair/generation categories cluster at
$Q_{\mathrm{HW}}{\approx}0.7576$ ($S{\approx}73.7$ with $E{=}0.973$).
The agent passes validation gates but performs no architecture-level
optimization on repair tasks.
(2)~Qwen3.6-27B dominates on optimization quality
(avg.\ $Q_{\mathrm{HW}}{=}0.812$, 60\% of optimization tasks $>$76, 52\%
$>$80), while Qwen3.5 achieves avg.\ $Q_{\mathrm{HW}}{=}0.775$
(28\% $>$76).
(3)~Model architecture appears more predictive of HLS repair success than
parameter count: the 27\,B dense model outperforms both 122\,B and 1.6\,T
MoE models on structural repair (100\% vs.\ 80\% and 92\%).

\takeaway{Qwen3.6-27B leads completion (\QwenThreeSixSuccessRate) and
optimization quality (\QwenThreeSixQorScore{} average QoR score on
optimization tasks), suggesting architecture matters more than parameter
count for HLS repair. Only explicit optimization tasks benefit from
LLM-driven QoR improvement; repair tasks achieve correctness without
quality gains.}
