\section{Completion Outpaces Score Coverage}
\label{sec:evaluation}

\textbf{Protocol.} Three hosted endpoints process the same balanced
\CorpusTaskCount{}-task suite under one agent revision and
\CampaignCreditLimit{}-credit limit. Each completed output must pass \CSim,
\Synth, 100 MHz, U55C capacity, and every applicable \CoSim{} gate under the
Track-A contract~\cite{fpt26tracka}. For tasks whose original records contain
failed API requests, the merged corpus selects an available submission-rerun
record and recomputes completion counts. Separate evaluator reruns fill
selected missing scores. ``Scored'' counts tasks with a non-null evaluator
score. The merged records retain \DeepSeekApiFailures{},
\QwenThreeFiveApiFailures{}, and \QwenThreeSixApiFailures{} failed requests,
respectively.
Appendix~\ref{app:benchmark} gives task provenance;
Appendix~\ref{app:results} gives category and selected-record accounting
details.

\textbf{Results.} DeepSeek provides the broadest and most balanced coverage:
\DeepSeekSuccessCount{}/\CorpusTaskCount{} completions and at least 24/25 in
every category. It also records the highest score sum and conditional mean.
Qwen3.5 trails by one completion, but its
\QwenThreeFiveScoredTaskCount{} scored tasks form the largest scored set.
The endpoint-specific scored subsets prevent these aggregates from
establishing a paired quality advantage.

Qwen3.6 reaches \QwenThreeSixSuccessCount{} completions, including 25/25 in
compile repair, synthesis repair, and QoR optimization. Its residual gap
concentrates in structural repair (18/25) and code generation (21/25).
Only \QwenThreeSixScoredTaskCount{} Qwen3.6 tasks carry evaluator scores, so
its score sum omits \QwenThreeSixUnscoredTaskCount{} corpus tasks.

\takeaway{Targeted reruns raise completion coverage across all three
endpoints. DeepSeek leads completion and score sum, while unequal scored
subsets prevent a paired quality claim.}
