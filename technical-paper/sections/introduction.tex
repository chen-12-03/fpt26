\section{Evidence Must Control LLM Edits}

HLS engineers must interpret unfamiliar C/C++ kernels, fix functional and
streaming failures, and tune hardware under a small evaluation budget. Vitis
HLS exposes correctness and performance only through simulation and synthesis
reports~\cite{amd2025vitis}. An LLM can propose source changes, but its text
cannot certify an interface, a register-transfer-level (RTL) execution, or a
hardware tradeoff.

LLM-assisted HLS systems automate code transformation and design-space
exploration~\cite{xiong2024hlspilot,xu2024ralad}. General tool-using agents
interleave model reasoning and actions~\cite{yao2023react}. A metered HLS loop
additionally requires a candidate-promotion rule. An unchecked edit can pass
compilation while failing a hidden test, deadlocking in RTL, missing 100 MHz,
or growing one resource beyond its speedup. Partial validation can also exhaust
the credits needed to recover.

We call our evidence-controlled promotion mechanism the Verified-Candidate
Loop (\VCL). The paper develops three claims. \textbf{(1) Validity
preservation:} the validation chain prevents candidates that fail any
available required gate from replacing the verified fallback.
\textbf{(2) QoR alignment:} the bounded hardware-quality score \QHW{} rejects
speedups that lose on clock or the worst-growing resource.
\textbf{(3) Evidence reuse:} QoR-guided retrieval-augmented generation
(QoR-RAG) and Failure Reflection focus later iterations on compatible actions
and prior failures. Section~\ref{sec:evaluation} maps each claim to a separate
experiment; the final revision will add measured results.
