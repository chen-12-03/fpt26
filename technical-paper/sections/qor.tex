\section{One Score Rejects False Speedups}

$Q_{\mathrm{HW}}$ combines performance gain and resource cost into a single
bounded utility. Let anchor $a$ and candidate $c$ have cycle latency $L$,
effective period $p$, and initiation interval $II$. The performance ratio
$r_P = (p_a L_a)/(p_c L_c)$ captures speedup; the area ratio $r_A$ uses a
capacity-normalized footprint $F(x)=\sum_{r} x_r/K_r$ over LUT, FF, DSP,
BRAM, and URAM to prevent discontinuous jumps from resource transfers. Two
evidence factors reward source edits ($D$, $1.01{\times}$) and validity
repair ($F$, $2{\times}$). The aggregate hardware ratio
$h = 1.01^{D}\,2^{F}\,r_P^{\,0.55}\,r_A^{\,0.45}$ weights performance at
55\% and area at 45\%, calibrated against 99 frozen PPA references.

$Q_{\mathrm{HW}} = 1-1/(1+h)^2$ maps $h{=}1$ (no change) to $0.75$,
$h{\to}\infty$ to $1$, and $h{\to}0$ to $0$, with continuous degradation
for bloated speedups. Budget efficiency
$E = \max(0.80,\,1-0.10u_{\mathrm{credit}}-0.10u_{\mathrm{time}})$
discounts excessive tool calls with a floor at $0.80$, where each $u$ is
the consumed fraction clipped to $[0,1]$. The final score is
\begin{equation}
S = 100\,V(c)\,Q_{\mathrm{HW}}\,E.
\label{eq:score}
\end{equation}
Promotion compares $Q_{\mathrm{HW}}$ only after $V(c){=}1$; a failed gate
yields $S{=}0$, and the efficiency floor guarantees a valid baseline at
least $60$ points.
